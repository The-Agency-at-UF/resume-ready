# Business impact metrics

Prioritize metrics that explain the value of the work to the client or organization. Select relevant outcomes rather than asking for every metric below.

## Measured business outcomes

- **Sales:** Additional purchases, sales volume, average order value, total deal value, or lead-to-sale conversion.
- **Revenue:** Additional revenue in dollars, revenue growth, or recurring revenue. Distinguish revenue from profit and pipeline value.
- **Customer acquisition:** New customers, paying accounts, qualified leads, or acquisition cost. Visits and inquiries are not customers.
- **Retention and churn:** Repeat purchases, renewals, retention rate, or reduced customer churn (customer turnover). Specify the customer cohort and period.
- **Donations:** Additional dollars raised, completed donations, donor conversion, recurring donors, or average gift. Distinguish total donations from incremental donations.
- **Efficiency and costs:** Measured staff hours saved, processing-time reduction, fewer errors, or actual software expenses avoided.
- **Community and education:** People served, resource access, participation, learning outcomes, or use of services. Prefer demonstrated benefit over audience size alone.

## Evidence to request

For a claimed improvement, identify:

- The before and after values, units, baseline, and comparison period.
- The source, such as analytics, sales reports, donation records, customer records, or benchmark results.
- Whether the number is a total, an increase, a target, or an estimate.
- The student's action, whether the feature was deployed during the measurement period, and evidence connecting the work to the outcome.
- Other team contributions or campaigns that affect attribution.

Retain a documented percentage as reported. When calculating a change from supplied values, make the calculation traceable in the Verify line and distinguish relative percent change from percentage-point change. A churn rate moving from 10% to 8% falls by 2 percentage points, or 20% relative to baseline.

Do not infer business gains from page counts, competitor benchmarks, or engagement alone. Use 'contributing to' only for supported shared results. When a result is unmeasured, frame it as a goal and request the evidence needed to verify it.

## Projects without measured business outcomes

Use confirmed scope and relevant intended value. Do not invent sales, customer, revenue, retention, or donation gains for an unlaunched project.

- **Websites:** Pages delivered, responsive layouts, components, accessibility issues fixed, signup/donation pathways, or measured task completion in usability tests.
- **AI automation:** Document types supported, rules automated, benchmark processing time, manual steps removed, or evaluated output quality.
- **Internal tools:** Integrations delivered, workflows automated, assets managed, supported accounts, or tested capacity. Capacity is not actual adoption.
- **Archives:** Records migrated, searchable categories, filters delivered, or retrieval time measured in testing.
- **Marketing sites:** Services explained, tutorials delivered, lead forms, conversion paths, or technical SEO checks. Traffic is not revenue.
- **Showcases:** Initiatives presented, institutional metrics displayed, or verified audience size. Presentation attendance is not investment secured.

Use X only for relevant missing values. Keep the bullet focused on the strongest one or two metrics and put necessary evidence requests in Verify rather than lengthening the bullet.
